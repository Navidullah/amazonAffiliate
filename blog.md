If you've ever tried to save a Facebook video or TikTok to your phone, you already know the frustration: the platforms deliberately remove or hide the download option for most content. Here's why — and why an online video downloader solves the problem instantly.

Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok restrict downloads for two main reasons:

1. **Keeping users on the platform** — If you can save a video and watch it offline, you don't need to open the app, which means fewer ad impressions for the platform.
2. **Copyright and creator control** — Creators can choose whether their content is downloadable. Many leave this option off by default, even for public videos.

The result is that even publicly posted videos — ones anyone can watch — often can't be saved with a simple tap. [EXTERNAL LINK: "According to Meta's developer documentation" → source: developers.facebook.com/docs]

A tool like **Shopyor** works by reading the video's direct source URL from the platform's publicly accessible content delivery network (CDN). When you paste a video link, Shopyor fetches that source file and gives you a direct download link — the same video file the platform uses to stream it to your screen, delivered straight to your device.

This is why it works even when the in-app download button is disabled or missing. You're not bypassing anything — you're simply accessing the public video file through a different route.

One common mistake: copying a shortened or share-card URL instead of the direct video link. On TikTok especially, sharing via DM or message sometimes generates a link that redirects to a page rather than the video itself. Always:

- Use the **"Copy Link"** button within the app's share menu
